Why podcast hosts need a separate speaker website

Most podcasts already have a web page. Your host probably gives you one, or you've built a simple site with episodes, a subscribe button and a contact link.

That page is great for listeners. It's not built for an event planner who has five minutes to decide whether you're right for a conference.

Here's what each visitor is looking for:

Visitor What they want What helps them act
Listener The latest episode, where to subscribe Play buttons and platform links
Sponsor Audience size and fit, ad options A media kit and contact details
Event planner What you speak about, how you come across on stage, how to book you Talk titles, a reel, testimonials and a booking form

If a planner lands on your episode page, they have to dig for the information they need. Many won't. A separate speaker page puts that information first, and it lets your podcast do what it does best: show that you have ideas worth hearing.

What event planners look for on a podcast host's website

Planners take a risk every time they book a speaker they haven't seen live. Your website's job is to lower that risk. In practice, that means answering five questions quickly.

1. What do you speak about?

Two to four talk titles, each with one sentence on what the audience will leave with. Name talks by the outcome, not by your show. "Why Good Nurses Leave, and What Keeps Them" is easier to book than "The Bedside Podcast, Live".

2. How do you come across?

A short video. Podcasters have an advantage here: if you record video episodes, you already have hours of footage of yourself explaining ideas clearly. Two minutes of your best moments is enough to start.

3. Who trusts you?

Notable guests, events you've spoken at, listener reviews and, most valuable of all, a testimonial from an event organiser.

4. What will it cost, and what's included?

Planners don't need an exact quote on your site, but "from" prices or a line on how fees work saves both of you a round of emails.

5. How do I book you?

A booking form that asks for the event date, location, audience and format. One click from anywhere on the page.

A good speaker website answers all five in the first scroll or two. For a full checklist, see what every speaker website needs.

How to present your podcast on a speaker website

Your show is a strong credential, but it shouldn't take over the page. Here's how to make it work for you.

Lead with your topic, not your show

Your headline should say what you speak about and who for. The podcast comes in your bio and its own short section further down.

Weak headline Stronger headline
Host of The Growth Room podcast Helping small business owners price their work with confidence
Podcaster, speaker, creator Keynotes on burnout and retention for hospital leaders
Welcome to my website Practical money conversations for teams and families

Turn your show into proof

In your podcast section, include:

  • What the show covers, in one or two sentences
  • Notable guests, as a short list of names planners might recognise
  • One or two listener reviews, quoted with a name or handle
  • Where to listen, with links to the main platforms
  • Audience numbers, only if they help. Share honest figures you're comfortable with anyone seeing. A specific audience, such as "a weekly show for procurement leaders", often impresses planners more than a big download number.

Use video episodes for your reel

If you record video, your reel can be built almost entirely from your episodes. Pick moments where you explain an idea, tell a story or ask a sharp question. Add any stage footage you have, even from a phone. Our guide shows how to make a two-minute speaker reel from footage you already have.

Offer formats only a host can

Podcast hosts can offer formats most speakers can't:

  • Live podcast recording: you interview a speaker or expert on stage, the audience asks questions and the event gets a published episode.
  • Panel moderator: you already know how to keep a conversation moving and bring out the best in guests.
  • Fireside chat host: a relaxed interview with an executive or guest of honour.

Listing these on your site gives planners a reason to book you even when they already have their keynote speakers.

How to link your podcast and your speaker site

Your show is the best promotion your speaker site will ever get. A few small habits send the right people to it.

  • Show notes. Add one line to every episode's notes: "Book [Name] to speak at your event: [link]". Over a year, that's dozens of links to your speaker page.
  • Your podcast website. Add a "Speaking" item to the menu that points to your speaker site.
  • On the show itself. Mention once in a while that you speak at events, and say where to find out more. Listeners who organise events, or work for companies that do, will remember.
  • Guest follow-ups. When you send a guest their episode link, add your speaker page to your email signature.
  • Social profiles. Put your speaker page in your LinkedIn featured section and your Instagram or X bio, alongside your podcast link.
  • Your speaker site, in return. Link back to your show, so planners can listen and hear how you think.

Keep your speaker page link short and easy to say out loud. "yourname.speakerfolio.com" works on air in a way a long URL never will.

Mistakes podcast hosts make with their websites

  • Sending planners to an episode list. It shows you're productive, not that you're bookable.
  • Leading with the show instead of the topic. Planners book outcomes for their audience.
  • Hiding the booking form. Put a button in the hero and the form at the bottom.
  • Using only listener reviews. Add at least one testimonial from an organiser as soon as you can.
  • Rounding up numbers. Planners and sponsors check. Honest, specific figures build trust.
  • No video. For a podcaster, this is the easiest proof to provide, so don't leave it out.
  • Letting it go stale. Add each new talk, event and testimonial as it happens.

How a speaker website helps you get found

Event planners search Google, and increasingly they ask AI assistants for speaker suggestions. Your podcast helps, but a dedicated speaker page makes it much clearer what you offer.

A good speaker site helps in three ways:

  • It matches what planners search for. Searches like "burnout speaker for nurses" or "small business pricing keynote" match a page that names your topics and audiences directly.
  • It gives AI assistants clear facts. Your name, topics, formats, location and past events, in plain text and structured data, are easier for assistants to understand and repeat.
  • It collects links from your show. Every episode's show notes can link to your speaker page, which helps search engines see it as important.
